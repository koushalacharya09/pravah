# Decisions

Format: Decision | Alternatives considered | Reason chosen | Trade-off accepted

## Ingestion

**Precedence needs both a 75% hit rate and a Wilson lower bound above 50%, with at least 5 co-observed rows.** | Keep an edge at 50%; require a significant binomial test; rank columns by mean date. | 50% is a coin flip, and a large file makes 52% "significant" without being a stage order. The mean date moves when one cell is a year late, and a stage that is usually blank looks early. The two bars do different jobs: 75% is the effect size, the lower bound is the certainty. | A perfect 5-row pair can pass (its lower bound is about 0.57) while 4 of 5 fails. Small tuition sheets will often be told the order is provisional. That is the point.

**A kept link with fewer than 73 co-observed rows is labelled provisional.** | Hide the sample size; bootstrap the proportion in this phase. | `n = 1.96² × 0.75 × 0.25 / 0.10² ≈ 73` is the Wald size for a ±10 point interval at the decision boundary. Bootstrap is a later phase and would make a deterministic proportion random. | The 73 is a planning number, not a guarantee that 73 rows make this particular business exchangeable.

**Headline rates use n = z² · p(1−p) / E², not a round number.** | Keep a hardcoded 100; use 30, the old normal rule of thumb. | With z = 1.96, p = 0.5 and E = 0.10 the formula is 96.04, so the next whole sample is 97. The code computes that. It is not stored as the literal 100. Prediction does not reuse it: a model wants 10 events per feature before it is worth fitting and 20 before it is comfortable, counted in cases because the outcome is one per case. | A rare event is precise sooner than the worst-case p = 0.5. We still plan for the worst case.

**Data quality and process readiness are separate scores.** | One health score that treats a missing timestamp as a failed table. | Missing cells, duplicate rows, mixed types, and outliers apply to every upload. Backwards time, unreadable event times, and single-event cases apply only when a case, an activity, and a time were all detected. A check that does not apply is labelled "Not applicable" and its weight is dropped, not charged. | A clean price list can score well on data quality and still be the wrong input for a process map. That refusal lives in readiness, not in a zero.

**Kahn's algorithm removes the earliest original column among zero-in-degree nodes, one node at a time.** | Depth-first topological sort; a binary heap. | The sheet's left-to-right order is a stable, visible tie-break. A heap is the same algorithm with more machinery than a 30-column graph needs. DFS hides the tie-break in the recursion stack. | Another valid order, which differs only on unconstrained stages, is not returned. The returned order is the stable one, not a uniquely true one.

**Cycles lose their weakest edge, one at a time, and the edge is reported.** | Refuse the file; solve the minimum feedback arc set; brute-force subsets while the column count is under 15. | A few backdated cells should not discard the sheet. Minimum feedback arc set minimises the number of deletions and can delete a strong edge. The rule a founder can check is "cross out the link the dates support least." | If the true link is slightly weaker than the false ones, the true link is the one removed. The note exists so a person can put it back.

**Shape is two hand-scored votes, not a classifier.** | A model trained on labelled workbooks; a dictionary of column names. | There is no labelled archive, and a wrong label has to be explainable. Names are not evidence: the same sheet arrives as `student_id` or as a header in another language. | Exactly two date columns plus a region and a status can look like a long log. A gap test (within-row gap versus between-row gap) breaks that tie, and it fails when a single activity legitimately lasts many days while the next event starts quickly.

**The two-date adjustment is 0.15.** | Leave the tie to whichever base score wins; use a larger nudge. | The base scores on that boundary differ by about 0.12. 0.15 reverses them when the gap evidence is clear and does nothing to a one-date log or a three-date sheet, because the adjustment only runs when there are exactly two date columns and both scores are already positive. | The number is a switch, not an estimated probability. Confidence is discounted when the final scores stay within 0.20.

**Case, activity, time, and resource are the highest score at or above 0.45. Ties keep the left-most column.** | Take the first column whose name matches a list; take the lowest-cardinality string as the activity. | A region is often the smallest string column and does not change inside a case. Coverage (how much of the catalogue one case visits) separates a stage list from a tutor. The cutoff leaves the role empty instead of filling it with a guess. | A process where each case does one activity out of many has low coverage and can lose the activity column. A single tutor who owns every step is penalised as a case attribute; if they are the only candidate the halved score may still clear 0.45.

**Anonymised columns are barred from case, activity, and time, and allowed as resource.** | Drop personal columns; hash everything that is high cardinality. | Dropping a tutor column removes the only resource. Hashing a case id would destroy the join. The hash includes the column name so a phone and an id with the same digits do not collide. | The default salt is in the source. Anyone with the salt can hash likely phone numbers and look for a match. Short numbers under 10 digits, and a bare first name with no name-like header, are kept, because both look like ordinary categories.

**Numeric columns are not dates.** | Accept Excel serials in the 20,000–60,000 band. | A case id and a price live in the same band once the workbook's cell type is gone. openpyxl already returns real Excel dates as datetimes. | A CSV of serial numbers exported as integers will not be read as dates. The founder re-exports them as dates.

**Ambiguous day-month values become missing when the column contains both conventions.** | Always day-first, because the product is used in India; always month-first. | One column that mixes `20/01/2024` and `01/20/2024` has no single convention. Guessing a third row of `05/06/2024` would invent a month. | A column that is entirely ambiguous used to be read month-first. The sheet-level rule below replaces that silent guess when several date columns can be compared.

**A fully ambiguous slash sheet is a hypothesis test, not a country setting.** | Keep the old month-first default; always use DD/MM because the users are in India. | A part above 12 is proof and wins on its own. When every slash date is `03/04/2026`, both calendars are possible. The format that makes more left-to-right column pairs go earlier to later is the one kept, and the gap between the two scores is the confidence. A gap under 0.05 is a tie: DD/MM is used and the confidence stays low, so the page does not pretend the format was observed. | The test assumes the date columns are written in process order. A sheet that stores the last stage in the first date column will pick the wrong calendar. One lone ambiguous column cannot be tested and falls through to the old month-first parse.

**Near-duplicate people are Levenshtein inside a block, before hashing.** | Exact string match; compare every name with every other name; compare after anonymisation. | Exact match misses "Rahul Sharma" and "Rahul Sharmaa". All-pairs is quadratic in the number of rows. A hash has no edit distance left. Rows are compared only when they share a phone, or the same first letter when there is no phone, and a block larger than 40 is skipped. The page reports row numbers and similarity, not the name. | Two short different names can still clear 0.84. A duplicated person with a mistyped phone and a different initial is missed. Exact same names on different phones are also reported when they share a first letter.

**Missingness is tested against the other columns, and only "not MCAR" is claimed.** | Report the percent missing; fit Little's MCAR test; fit a logistic regression of the flag. | A percent does not say whether the filled average is biased. Little's test wants a normal covariance these mixed sheets do not have. Chi-square (categories, expected count at least 5) and Mann–Whitney (numbers, at least 8 a side) are enough to see a flag that lines up with an observed column. Benjamini–Hochberg at 0.05 keeps a scan over many columns from inventing hits. | A column can still be missing for a reason that is not in the file. That is not declared MAR or MNAR. Rows from the same case are treated as independent, so an event log can overstate the evidence. The test runs on the sheet before melting, because a blank stage date disappears once the row is dropped.

**Extreme numbers use a modified z-score, not `(x - mean) / sd`.** | Ordinary z-score; Isolation Forest. | One huge fee moves the mean and the standard deviation toward itself, so the next outlier looks ordinary. The median and the median absolute deviation stay with the bulk. The score is `0.6745 * (x - median) / MAD`, and `|z| > 3.5` is the flag. When MAD is 0, because more than half the values are identical, the 1.5 IQR fence is used instead. | A column that is legitimately two tight groups can flag the smaller group. Fewer than 8 numbers are not scored.

**Each column pair gets the association that matches its type.** | Pearson for everything; mutual information for everything. | Two numbers that rise together on a curve need Spearman, which is Pearson on ranks. Two categories need Cramér's V, because a raw chi-square grows with the sample. A number split by a label needs the correlation ratio, because the label has no order to rank. Mutual information is kept as the backup and as a second number, in bits. | Identifiers and free text are excluded. At most 8 columns are shown. Cramér's V is withheld when an expected count is under 5.

**Benford's law is only for amounts that can obey it.** | Run it on every numeric column; never run it. | First digits of a naturally varying positive amount follow `log10(1 + 1/d)`. A fixed fee list does not, and accusing it is a false alarm. The column is skipped, with a reason, when there are fewer than 30 positive values, 8 or fewer distinct values, or the largest is not at least 10 times the smallest. A tested column uses chi-square on 8 degrees of freedom. | The tail probability is the Wilson–Hilferty approximation, not the regularised gamma. A borderline p-value can move. Dependence between rows makes the departure look sharper than it is.

**The readiness table is a rule, and the safe-to-analyse band is the health score plus the 100-row floor.** | Let every later page try and fail; ask a model which analysis to run. | Process map needs an ordered log. Survival needs cases of different lengths, which is the censoring this file can actually show. Prediction is refused under 100 rows and marked caution under 300. Forecast is refused under 8 weeks and caution under 16. Causes is Caution “Choose a treatment to unlock.” when a case, an activity, and a time exist, and No otherwise. The band is Yes only at health ≥ 80 and n ≥ 100; Caution from 50 up; No below 50. | Equal case lengths can hide open cases, so survival is then refused. Sixteen weeks is a minimum, not a seasonal model.

**Wide-sheet health is measured in stage order, then the working table is sorted by time.** | Sort first and then score; score the sorted table only. | Sorting first would hide the backdated cell the precedence step just found. Keeping the file order forever would hand later phases a log that is not chronological. | After a successful rebuild the working frame and the scored frame are not the same object. The inversion count lives on the validation result, not in the sorted frame.

**The health score has no interval of its own.** | Add the Wilson variances; bootstrap the score now. | The five rates share rows, so the sum of variances is too small. Bootstrap belongs to the statistics phase. Each rate already carries its interval, and the sample-size sentence states the 100-row floor. | A founder who reads "82" may still treat it as exact. The sentence under it is the correction.

**Weights are 25 missing, 25 unreadable time, 20 backwards time, 15 duplicates, 15 single-event cases.** | Equal weights; a single "share of clean rows." | Time is half the score because every later phase orders on it. A file of one event per case can be perfectly clean and still have no process; that failure needs its own term, but not half the score, because some businesses really are one step. | The weights are a judgement. They are written down so they can be argued with. They are not fitted.

**pm4py is optional and used only to read XES.** | Require pm4py; reimplement discovery with it. | The spec allows pm4py for XES input and forbids it as the mining engine. A builtin reader keeps ingestion installable on a machine where pm4py's stack does not build. | The builtin reader keeps one level of event attributes. Nested XES containers are out of scope. When both readers exist, tests check the attributes a person can count by hand, not pm4py's extra classifier columns.

**The shareable page is a Flask app on Vercel, not Streamlit.** | Wait for the phase-10 Streamlit dashboard; rewrite ingestion in JavaScript so a static site can run it. | Vercel runs one Python process and can serve a link today. Streamlit needs a long-lived server Vercel does not keep. The page only calls `ingest` and prints the computed narrative. The analysis is still the Python engine. | The first request after a quiet period is slow, because pandas has to load. Uploads are capped at 3 MB, under Vercel's request limit. There is still no process map or risk list.

**Ingestion draws no random numbers.** | Seed a hash or a sample inside the loader. | The same file must produce the same table. `RANDOM_SEED = 42` lives in one module so later draws do not invent a second seed. | The bottleneck bootstrap, the model arena, the propensity bootstrap, and the simulation replications all read that seed. Ingestion itself still does not.

## Survival, risk, causes, simulation, plan

**Open cases stay in the Kaplan-Meier risk set.** | Drop unfinished cases; treat the last day as the event. | Dropout and completion are selected by the reader. The default completion is the modal last activity. Dropout is silence past a threshold, censored if the completion activity was reached. The band is Greenwood's log-log interval.

**The Cox ratio is withheld when any Schoenfeld p-value is under 0.05.** | Always print the hazard ratio; fit a time-varying coefficient. | A crossed hazard does not have one ratio. Stratified Kaplan-Meier curves remain. Log-rank tests against the largest level use Benjamini-Hochberg when more than one comparison is made.

**A risk model trains only with 10 finished prefix rows per feature, and only on facts already observed.** | Train on the whole case; always show an AUC. | The event-log sample has too few events, so the page says no model was trained. The winner is PR-AUC when the rarer class is under 40%, otherwise ROC-AUC. Shapley values are exact against the training mean. Remaining-time intervals are split conformal at 90%.

**"Likely causal" requires balance under 0.1 and every refutation.** | Call the IPW number causal because a diagram was drawn. | Placebo, a random common cause, a 75% subset, and an E-value of at least 1.2 all have to pass, and the interval has to exclude 0. Otherwise the verdict is association only, with the failed check named. Treatments are generated from the activities in the file.

**What-if numbers exist only after the baseline reproduces the file.** | Always show the scenario median. | The tolerance is 35% on median throughput time and 0.20 on stage shares. A miss blocks the scenario and leaves the action plan with no gain. Sixteen replications report a 90% interval. The weekly forecast replaces the seasonal naive only when its error is lower.

**The action plan is a 0/1 knapsack on simulated gains.** | Sort by value per cost and stop. | The classic 60/100/120 against costs 10/20/30 inside budget 50 is why: greedy scores 160 and the program scores 220. The risk-adjusted option uses the lower end of the gain interval. A sentence is copied from a module that already computed it.

## Bottlenecks

**The rank is the share of throughput time spent waiting in front of an activity, not the mean wait.** | Rank by mean waiting time; rank by the longest single gap. | A rare step with one long gap is not where the organisation loses its days. The share is `sum of waits / sum of throughput times`. With one timestamp the gap is called transition time and processing time is left blank, because the file does not contain a service time. | The first activity always has a zero share. A step that is slow to perform but has no queue does not win the rank; its processing time is shown beside the bar instead.

**The bootstrap interval resamples cases, 1000 times, seed 42, and reports percentiles.** | A normal interval around the share; resample events. | Events in one case are not independent, so resampling rows would invent precision. A normal interval stays positive when every case is a copy, which is a lie. Percentiles of the case resample are zero-width in that case. | 1000 draws is a Monte Carlo error of its own. The seed makes the published interval repeatable, not exact.

**Little's Law is reported, and the steady-state flag is the two halves of the window.** | Compare `λW` with the time-average and flag a gap; run a stationarity test. | On a window that starts and ends with the completed visits, `λW` equals the time-average. That comparison cannot fail. Splitting the window in half shows a queue that grew. The flag is a relative gap above 25% between the halves. Fewer than 5 completed visits are not checked. | A slow seasonal swing inside one window can be flagged. The sentence says the step is not in steady state. It does not say why the load changed.

**A resource median is shrunk with prior strength 5, and a resource under 5 events cannot outrank a larger one.** | Rank the raw median; estimate the prior variance from the group means. | One slow case makes a new tutor the worst resource. The weight `n / (n + 5)` is the normal-normal weight when the prior is worth 5 observations. Estimating the prior variance from the groups lets that one case inflate the variance and escape the shrinkage. The rank cap is the second lock. | A tutor who is truly much slower, with only four events, stays off the top until a fifth event arrives.

**One slowdown date, from binary segmentation on the weekly median.** | CUSUM; several change-points. | The question is "when did it get slower", which is one date. The split must leave two weeks on each side, the later mean must be higher, and the residual sum of squares must fall by at least 20%. CUSUM needs a threshold in standard errors that four weeks cannot estimate. | A gradual drift can miss the 20% bar. No date is then named, which is preferred to naming a noisy week.

**Rework cost is the span from the first repeat to the last, not the whole throughput time.** | Count the loop and stop; charge every day of the case. | The first pass would have happened anyway. The days from the first time the activity occurs to the last time are the extra calendar time. | Activities in between are inside that span. A loop that uses two different labels is missed.
